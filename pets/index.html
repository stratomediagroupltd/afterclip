The camera

A real camera.
Already rolling.

Every setting a serious camera app gives you, on the iPhones that capture it. And it has been recording the whole time.

  1. Capture quality

    Up to 4K, with HDR.

    Pick the lens, the resolution, the frame rate and the bitrate, and turn HDR on. Everything a serious camera app gives you, and it never stops keeping the last seconds.

  2. Frame rate

    Up to 240 frames a second.

    24 for a film look, 60 for sport, 120 or 240 for slow motion. You choose the rate, the resolution and the lens yourself, and the camera runs exactly as you set it.

  3. Stabilization

    Steady, even when you aren't.

    Standard reacts instantly, which suits a replay buffer best. The cinematic modes are smoother still, for when you're walking, running or laughing.

  4. Bitrate

    Detail, or space. Your call.

    Leave it on Auto, or choose how much data each second of video gets, and see what a minute of it will take before you change it.

  5. Presets

    Your setups, one tap away.

    A preset remembers your capture quality, clip cleanup and accent. Switch between everyday, sports and low light in one tap, from your iPhone or your watch.

  6. Tools

    Controls where you want them.

    Every round control on the capture screen is a tool you choose. Put the ones you use on screen, in your order, and hide the rest.

FAQ

answered.

What's the best way to film my cat or dog doing tricks?

Leave AfterClip open on a stand at their eye level and just play. Because it keeps the last seconds, you tap Save after they do the trick, so you never have to guess when to press record.

Can I film my pet in slow motion?

Yes. Choose 120 fps, or 240 fps on iPhones that film it, and Photos plays the saved clip in slow motion.

Will it fill up my iPhone?

No. AfterClip keeps only the last seconds you choose and overwrites the rest, so it can stay open for a whole play session. Only the moments you save go into Photos.

When can I get AfterClip?

AfterClip is coming soon to the App Store, for iPhone and Apple Watch. Follow @afterclipapp on Instagram to hear the moment it's out.

How does it work?

While AfterClip is open, it keeps the last seconds of video and sound in a buffer on your iPhone. When something happens, tap Save and you get the seconds before the tap. You choose how far back it reaches, from 10 seconds to 30 minutes.

Does it record when the app is closed?

No. iOS lets a camera record only while its app is on screen, so open AfterClip when you want to be ready. The Lock Screen, Control Center and the Action button get you there fastest.

Where do my clips go? Is anything uploaded?

Straight to your Photos library. Nothing is uploaded, and AfterClip collects no data. The buffer stays in the app's own storage on your iPhone and is constantly overwritten.

How long can a clip be?

As long as the buffer, up to 30 minutes. The dial above the shutter sets how many seconds a tap keeps, and you can change it right before you save.

What if I save twice during the same moment?

The clips join into one, with no footage saved twice. Clips that a newer clip already contains are removed for you, and iOS asks you to confirm first.

Does it use a lot of battery or storage?

It records while it's open, so it uses about as much battery as filming. To save power, it can hide the preview when you haven't touched the screen for a while. The buffer takes about 70 MB per minute at 1080p and 30 fps, and it's overwritten as it goes, so it never grows.

What happens to the buffer if I close the app?

It's kept. Reopen AfterClip and the footage it was holding is still there to save.

What does the Apple Watch app do?

It's a remote for your iPhone's camera. Open AfterClip on your iPhone, keep it nearby, and the watch shows a live preview: tap to focus, hold for AE/AF lock, turn the Digital Crown to zoom, and save, switch lens or change quality.

Can I save with the volume buttons, a remote or a game controller?

Yes. In AfterClip, open Settings → Buttons → Assign a button, then press the button you want to use. A press and a hold can do different things.

Which devices does it run on?

iPhone with iOS 18.2 or later, and Apple Watch with watchOS 11.2 or later. 4K, HDR, slow motion and extra lenses depend on what your iPhone's cameras can do.
